# Background

- On 1 September 2008, Manchester City agreed a takeover by the Abu Dhabi United Group, headed by Sheikh Mansour. The takeover brought a major increase in the club's financial resources and was followed immediately by significant spending on players.
    - `Manchester City have agreed a takeover deal with the Abu Dhabi United Group for Development and Investment (Adug), the club has confirmed.` — [Manchester City agree takeover deal with Abu Dhabi group, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/01/manchestercity.premierleague)
    - `This morning City find themselves not only the richest club in Manchester but perhaps the world. The announcement yesterday that Thaksin Shinawatra, the former prime minister of Thailand, had sold the club to Abu Dhabi United Group (Adug) is expected to be followed by the biggest injection of cash into a football club since Roman Abramovich changed the face of the English game with his £600m-plus investment in Chelsea.` — [You're bought! Abu Dhabi's answer to Sir Alan Sugar in Manchester City takeover, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/02/manchestercity.premierleague)
- On 1 September 2008, City demonstrated the scale of its new spending power by signing Robinho from Real Madrid for a British-record £32.5m on the same day as the takeover was announced.
    - `Manchester City demonstrated their new-found status as the richest club on the planet when the billionaire Arabs who have bought the club from Thaksin Shinawatra stunned Roman Abramovich late last night by signing Robinho from under the noses of Chelsea.` — [City beat Chelsea to sign £32.5m Robinho, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/02/manchestercity.chelsea1)
- On 17 September 2009, UEFA president Michel Platini publicly criticized the scale of Manchester City's spending following the takeover, arguing that such expenditure could contribute to inflation across European football.
    - `The Uefa president, Michel Platini, has singled out the expenditure at City since the takeover by Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed al-Nahyan, a senior member of the Abu Dhabi royal family, a year ago. City have spent more than £200m on 11 players since, which Uefa has condemned as an inflationary danger for clubs across European football.` — [Abu Dhabi takeover of Manchester City 'no danger to football', insists chairman, The Guardian, 2009](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2009/sep/17/manchester-city-abu-dhabi-takeover)
- On 21 September 2009, UEFA's Executive Committee approved the Financial Fair Play concept. The stated objectives included improving financial stability, encouraging clubs to operate within their revenues and introducing greater financial discipline.
    - `The major objective of the Financial Fair Play concept is to improve the financial fairness in European competitions, as well as the long-term stability of European club football. In order to achieve this goal, a set of measures will be put in place. These include an obligation for clubs whose turnover is over a certain threshold, over a period of time, to balance their books or break even.` — [UEFA EXCO approves financial fair play, UEFA, 2009](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0254-0d7c87e72463-23e4c100b95f-1000/)
    - `UEFA President Michel Platini said: "The idea is not to hurt clubs. The idea is to help them. The basic premise is that clubs should not spend more than they earn. Club owners have asked for the introduction of rules, and this will be an adventure for European football and UEFA." Michel Platini said the measures were essential for the long-term health of the European game.` — [UEFA EXCO approves financial fair play, UEFA, 2009](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0254-0d7c87e72463-23e4c100b95f-1000/)
- On 22 September 2009, Sheikh Mansour completed the purchase of the remaining 10% of Manchester City's shares, giving him 100% ownership of the club. By that point, more than £200m had already been spent on players and other investment since the takeover.
    - `Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed al-Nahyan of the Abu Dhabi ruling family has bought the 10% of Manchester City not previously under his control, completing his acquisition of 100% of the club's shares, a year since he dramatically bought his 90% majority shareholding.`
      
      `Since that takeover by Mansour's Abu Dhabi United Group, more than £200m has been spent on new players and other investment in a revolution at City, all conducted with 10% of the shares remaining outside his ownership.`
      
      `Mansour paid £210m to the former Thai prime minister Thaksin Shinawatra, for 90% of the club, after Thaksin had initially hoped to retain a greater share than 10% and enter a partnership with Mansour.`
      
      `Thaksin is now not considered a fit and proper person to be a Premier League football club director or 30% shareholder, since his conviction of corruption in Thailand last October. After the takeover Thaksin was widely reported to have retained his interest in the remaining 10% of shares, and appointed as his nominees his long-term associate Jack Srisumrid, a Thai businessman, and the Greek shipping tycoon Victor Restis. Srisumrid, 38, became a City director in August 2007 shortly after Thaksin bought the club. Restis, 41, based in Athens, was appointed to the City board after Mansour's takeover but is understood to have been a representative of the 10% holding, not of Mansour's 90%.`
      
      `Both Srisumrid and Restis resigned as directors of City last Friday and the club announced last night the purchase of the 10% shareholding reportedly held by Thaksin, "for an undisclosed sum". A club statement said: "As a result of the transaction, Manchester City is now 100% owned by his highness Sheikh Mansour."` — [Last shares give Sheikh Mansour total control of Manchester City, The Guardian, 2009](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2009/sep/23/manchester-city-takeover)
- On 27 May 2010, UEFA formally approved the Club Licensing and Financial Fair Play Regulations. The regulations were phased in over three years, with the break-even requirement applying to financial statements ending in 2012 and being assessed during the 2013/14 UEFA club competition season.
    - `UEFA's Executive Committee has today unanimously approved the new UEFA Club Licensing and Financial Fair Play Regulations, submitted to the committee with the support of all stakeholders in European football.`
      
      `After a phased implementation over three years – 2010, 2011 and 2012 – the main cornerstone of the regulations, the break-even requirement, enters into force for the financial statements of the reporting period ending 2012, to be assessed during the 2013/14 UEFA club competition season.`
      
      `Commenting on this key decision for the future well-being of European football, UEFA president Michel Platini said: "We have worked on the financial fair play concept hand-in-hand with the clubs, as our intention is not to punish them but to protect them. We have an agreement with the clubs. The philosophy is that you cannot spend more money than you generate.` — [Financial Fair Play Regulations are approved, UEFA, 2010](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/01e5-0ea1848137bd-b1291315b87e-1000--financial-fair-play-regulations-are-approved/)
- On 31 May 2012, Manchester City's 2011/12 financial year ended with the club recording a loss of £97.9m. City nevertheless stated that it remained confident of meeting UEFA's FFP requirements, arguing that certain expenditure could be excluded from the break-even calculation.
    - `Manchester City have announced annual losses of £97.9m. Although the sum is still enormous, it is less than half the £197.5m from 12 months' ago.`
      
      `And with City reporting turnover in the 2011-12 season of £231.1m, the highest in the club's history, it underlines the rapid progress being made by the Premier League champions in establishing themselves as one of Europe's elite clubs.`
      
      `Although failure to progress from the Champions League group stage has had an obvious impact on turnover, this year and last, the club are still delighted with the performance.`
      
      `And they are confident even the huge losses confirmed on Friday will not have an impact on their ability to meet Uefa's strict Financial Fair Play guidelines as £15m comes from infrastructure and youth development costs. More importantly, approximately £80m comes from contracts that pre-date 2010, from which City expect to get some kind of relief.` — [Manchester City announce annual losses of £97.9m for 2011-12, The Guardian, 2012](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2012/dec/14/manchester-city-losses-97-million)

# Aftermath

## Outcome

- UEFA's 2014 action against Manchester City was an FFP investigation followed by a negotiated settlement, rather than a criminal or court charge.
    
- The settlement required a €60m total financial contribution, with €40m conditional on compliance.
    
- City's Champions League squad was restricted to 21 players for the relevant season.
    
- City accepted restrictions on transfer spending, its relevant wage bill and permitted losses.
    
- The agreement imposed continuing monitoring, with non-compliance capable of referral to UEFA's CFCB Adjudicatory Chamber.
    
- City maintained that it had complied with the rules according to its interpretation, but accepted the settlement rather than continuing to the adjudicatory stage.
