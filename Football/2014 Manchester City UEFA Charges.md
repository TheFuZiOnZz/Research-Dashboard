# Background

- On 1 September 2008, Manchester City agreed a takeover by the Abu Dhabi United Group, headed by Sheikh Mansour. The takeover brought a major increase in the club's financial resources and was followed immediately by significant spending on players.
    - `Manchester City have agreed a takeover deal with the Abu Dhabi United Group for Development and Investment (Adug), the club has confirmed.` — [Manchester City agree takeover deal with Abu Dhabi group, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/01/manchestercity.premierleague)
    - `This morning City find themselves not only the richest club in Manchester but perhaps the world. The announcement yesterday that Thaksin Shinawatra, the former prime minister of Thailand, had sold the club to Abu Dhabi United Group (Adug) is expected to be followed by the biggest injection of cash into a football club since Roman Abramovich changed the face of the English game with his £600m-plus investment in Chelsea.` — [You're bought! Abu Dhabi's answer to Sir Alan Sugar in Manchester City takeover, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/02/manchestercity.premierleague)
- On 1 September 2008, City demonstrated the scale of its new spending power by signing Robinho from Real Madrid for a British-record £32.5m on the same day as the takeover was announced.
    - `Manchester City demonstrated their new-found status as the richest club on the planet when the billionaire Arabs who have bought the club from Thaksin Shinawatra stunned Roman Abramovich late last night by signing Robinho from under the noses of Chelsea.` — [City beat Chelsea to sign £32.5m Robinho, The Guardian, 2008](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2008/sep/02/manchestercity.chelsea1)
- On 17 September 2009, UEFA president Michel Platini publicly criticized the scale of Manchester City's spending following the takeover, arguing that such expenditure could contribute to inflation across European football.
    - `The Uefa president, Michel Platini, has singled out the expenditure at City since the takeover by Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed al-Nahyan, a senior member of the Abu Dhabi royal family, a year ago. City have spent more than £200m on 11 players since, which Uefa has condemned as an inflationary danger for clubs across European football.` — [Abu Dhabi takeover of Manchester City 'no danger to football', insists chairman, The Guardian, 2009](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2009/sep/17/manchester-city-abu-dhabi-takeover)
- On 21 September 2009, UEFA's Executive Committee approved the Financial Fair Play concept. The stated objectives included improving financial stability, encouraging clubs to operate within their revenues and introducing greater financial discipline.
    - `The major objective of the Financial Fair Play concept is to improve the financial fairness in European competitions, as well as the long-term stability of European club football. In order to achieve this goal, a set of measures will be put in place. These include an obligation for clubs whose turnover is over a certain threshold, over a period of time, to balance their books or break even.` — [UEFA EXCO approves financial fair play, UEFA, 2009](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0254-0d7c87e72463-23e4c100b95f-1000/)
    - `UEFA President Michel Platini said: "The idea is not to hurt clubs. The idea is to help them. The basic premise is that clubs should not spend more than they earn. Club owners have asked for the introduction of rules, and this will be an adventure for European football and UEFA." Michel Platini said the measures were essential for the long-term health of the European game.` — [UEFA EXCO approves financial fair play, UEFA, 2009](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/0254-0d7c87e72463-23e4c100b95f-1000/)
- On 22 September 2009, Sheikh Mansour completed the purchase of the remaining 10% of Manchester City's shares, giving him 100% ownership of the club. By that point, more than £200m had already been spent on players and other investment since the takeover.
    - `Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed al-Nahyan of the Abu Dhabi ruling family has bought the 10% of Manchester City not previously under his control, completing his acquisition of 100% of the club's shares, a year since he dramatically bought his 90% majority shareholding.`
      
      `Since that takeover by Mansour's Abu Dhabi United Group, more than £200m has been spent on new players and other investment in a revolution at City, all conducted with 10% of the shares remaining outside his ownership.`
      
      `Mansour paid £210m to the former Thai prime minister Thaksin Shinawatra, for 90% of the club, after Thaksin had initially hoped to retain a greater share than 10% and enter a partnership with Mansour.`
      
      `Thaksin is now not considered a fit and proper person to be a Premier League football club director or 30% shareholder, since his conviction of corruption in Thailand last October. After the takeover Thaksin was widely reported to have retained his interest in the remaining 10% of shares, and appointed as his nominees his long-term associate Jack Srisumrid, a Thai businessman, and the Greek shipping tycoon Victor Restis. Srisumrid, 38, became a City director in August 2007 shortly after Thaksin bought the club. Restis, 41, based in Athens, was appointed to the City board after Mansour's takeover but is understood to have been a representative of the 10% holding, not of Mansour's 90%.`
      
      `Both Srisumrid and Restis resigned as directors of City last Friday and the club announced last night the purchase of the 10% shareholding reportedly held by Thaksin, "for an undisclosed sum". A club statement said: "As a result of the transaction, Manchester City is now 100% owned by his highness Sheikh Mansour."` — [Last shares give Sheikh Mansour total control of Manchester City, The Guardian, 2009](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2009/sep/23/manchester-city-takeover)
- On 27 May 2010, UEFA formally approved the Club Licensing and Financial Fair Play Regulations. The regulations were phased in over three years, with the break-even requirement applying to financial statements ending in 2012 and being assessed during the 2013/14 UEFA club competition season.
    - `UEFA's Executive Committee has today unanimously approved the new UEFA Club Licensing and Financial Fair Play Regulations, submitted to the committee with the support of all stakeholders in European football.`
      
      `After a phased implementation over three years – 2010, 2011 and 2012 – the main cornerstone of the regulations, the break-even requirement, enters into force for the financial statements of the reporting period ending 2012, to be assessed during the 2013/14 UEFA club competition season.`
      
      `Commenting on this key decision for the future well-being of European football, UEFA president Michel Platini said: "We have worked on the financial fair play concept hand-in-hand with the clubs, as our intention is not to punish them but to protect them. We have an agreement with the clubs. The philosophy is that you cannot spend more money than you generate.` — [Financial Fair Play Regulations are approved, UEFA, 2010](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/01e5-0ea1848137bd-b1291315b87e-1000--financial-fair-play-regulations-are-approved/)
- On 31 May 2012, Manchester City's 2011/12 financial year ended with the club recording a loss of £97.9m. City nevertheless stated that it remained confident of meeting UEFA's FFP requirements, arguing that certain expenditure could be excluded from the break-even calculation.
    - `Manchester City have announced annual losses of £97.9m. Although the sum is still enormous, it is less than half the £197.5m from 12 months' ago.`
      
      `And with City reporting turnover in the 2011-12 season of £231.1m, the highest in the club's history, it underlines the rapid progress being made by the Premier League champions in establishing themselves as one of Europe's elite clubs.`
      
      `Although failure to progress from the Champions League group stage has had an obvious impact on turnover, this year and last, the club are still delighted with the performance.`
      
      `And they are confident even the huge losses confirmed on Friday will not have an impact on their ability to meet Uefa's strict Financial Fair Play guidelines as £15m comes from infrastructure and youth development costs. More importantly, approximately £80m comes from contracts that pre-date 2010, from which City expect to get some kind of relief.` — [Manchester City announce annual losses of £97.9m for 2011-12, The Guardian, 2012](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2012/dec/14/manchester-city-losses-97-million)

    - `Manchester City have announced a loss of £52m for the year to 31 May 2013, a reduction from the £98m loss made by the club the previous year when winning the Premier League. # The Charges

- On 14 April 2014, Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain were among 76 clubs whose UEFA Financial Control Body cases were being considered. Clubs deemed to have committed serious breaches could be referred to the adjudicatory panel for a final decision.
    - `Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain are among the European clubs who will learn this week whether they are deemed to be in serious breach of Uefa's financial fair play rules.` — [Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain face financial fair play fate, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/14/manchester-city-psg-ffp-uefa-set-learn-fate)
    - `Those considered to have committed serious breaches of Uefa's break-even rules will be referred to the CFCB's adjudicatory panel for a final verdict.` — [Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain face financial fair play fate, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/14/manchester-city-psg-ffp-uefa-set-learn-fate)
- On 15 April 2014, Manchester City said it was confident that its finances were in order as UEFA prepared to decide whether sanctions were necessary. City had reported losses of £51.6m for 2012-13, although the club expected to reach break-even in the near future.
    - `Manchester City are confident their finances are in good order as Uefa prepares to rule if any club should face sanctions under its financial fair play regulations.` — [Manchester City confident of satisfying Uefa financial fair play rules, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/15/manchester-city-uefa-financial-fair-play-rules)
    - `While City have made total losses of around £150m in the past two seasons, the trend is downwards with the sum twice being halved.` — [Manchester City confident of satisfying Uefa financial fair play rules, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/15/manchester-city-uefa-financial-fair-play-rules)
- On 28 April 2014, UEFA made settlement offers to clubs it considered to have breached the Financial Fair Play rules, including Manchester City. Clubs could accept the proposed sanctions or negotiate; unresolved cases would be referred to the adjudicatory panel.
    - `Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain are among a number of clubs who have been offered settlements for breaching financial fair play rules.` — [Manchester City and PSG offered deals over financial fair play, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/28/manchester-city-financial-fair-play-settlements-offer)
    - `If no agreement is reached, the outstanding cases will go to an adjudicatory panel for a final decision.` — [Manchester City and PSG offered deals over financial fair play, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/28/manchester-city-financial-fair-play-settlements-offer)
- On 28 April 2014, BBC Sport reported that Manchester City was facing sanctions after UEFA determined that it had breached the FFP rules. The proposed settlement was understood to involve a fine and restrictions on City's European squad.
    - `Manchester City and Paris St-Germain are facing sanctions after breaching Uefa's financial fair play (FFP) rules.` — [Manchester City and PSG breach Uefa FFP rules, BBC Sport, 2014](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/27198306)
    - `City must now decide whether to accept the offer made to them or try to negotiate a lesser punishment, which varies from a reprimand to a fine or European squad restrictions next term.` — [Manchester City and PSG breach Uefa FFP rules, BBC Sport, 2014](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/27198306)
- On 2 May 2014, UEFA confirmed that nine clubs remained under investigation following concerns over compliance with its Financial Fair Play rules. Manchester City was widely reported to be one of the clubs involved.
    - `Uefa has confirmed that nine clubs, believed to include Manchester City and Paris Saint-Germain, remain at risk of punishment under its financial fair play rules designed to curb overspending.` — [Manchester City and PSG at risk of sanctions over financial fair play, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/02/manchester-city-psg-uefa-financial-fair-play)
    - `The investigatory chamber of Uefa's Club Financial Control Body met in Nyon over Thursday and Friday amid negotiations with the nine unidentified clubs.` — [Manchester City and PSG at risk of sanctions over financial fair play, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/02/manchester-city-psg-uefa-financial-fair-play)
- On 5 May 2014, Manchester City had not reached an agreement with UEFA over the proposed sanctions. The club was given until the end of the week to agree a settlement or have the case referred to the adjudicatory panel.
    - `Manchester City are still a long way off agreeing a settlement with Uefa over sanctions for breaking financial fair play rules.` — [Manchester City yet to agree punishment with Uefa over FFP, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/05/manchester-city-settlement-uefa-financial-fair-play)
    - `The club have until the end of the week to agree to the sanctions or face the case being handed to a panel for a non-negotiable decision.` — [Manchester City yet to agree punishment with Uefa over FFP, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/05/manchester-city-settlement-uefa-financial-fair-play)
- On 6 May 2014, details of the proposed UEFA punishment were reported, including a £49m fine, a possible reduction of City's Champions League squad to 21 players and a restriction on the club's wage bill.
    - `Manchester City are facing Uefa sanctions for breaching financial fair play rules which would see the club limited to a 21-man Champions League squad instead of the usual 25 players next season.` — [Manchester City face £49m fine and wage cap for financial fair play breach, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/06/manchester-city-face-49m-fine-wage-cap-financial-fair-play)
- On 6 May 2014, Manchester City was reported to be considering whether to accept UEFA's proposed settlement or challenge the sanctions through UEFA's adjudicatory process.
    - `Manchester City, furious at being bracketed with spendthrift Paris Saint-Germain, are considering taking on Uefa's new financial fair play regime after it emerged both clubs face huge fines of almost £50m and restrictions on their big-money squads.` — [Manchester City may yet challenge Uefa's £50m FFP fine and squad limit, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/06/manchester-city-consider-challenging-uefa-penalties)
    - `Manchester City must decide whether to reluctantly accept the punishment on the table or face the matter being referred to the club financial control body's adjudicatory chamber.` — [Manchester City may yet challenge Uefa's £50m FFP fine and squad limit, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/06/manchester-city-consider-challenging-uefa-penalties)
- On 10 May 2014, Manchester City did not accept UEFA's settlement by the deadline. The proposed agreement was reported to involve a €60m fine over three years, a 21-player Champions League squad and a wage cap. City maintained that it had not breached the rules.
    - `Uefa set a deadline of Friday for City to accept a €60m (£49m) settlement to be paid to the European governing body over three years, a 21-player limit to next season's Champions League squad and a wage cap to the current level.` — [Manchester City take the lead to face down Uefa over break-even rules, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/david-conn-inside-sport-blog/2014/may/10/manchester-city-face-down-uefa-financial-fair-play)

- On 13 May 2014, Manchester City had still not reached a settlement with UEFA. UEFA had made what was described as a `take it or leave it` offer, while the club remained opposed to the proposed sanctions.
    - `Manchester City have not yet reached a settlement with Uefa over the proposed sanctions they face for breaking financial fair play rules.` — [Manchester City's financial fair play deal is still on the rocks, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/13/manchester-city-financial-fair-play-deal)
    - `Uefa had hoped that Manchester City would agree to a fine of €60m (£49m) and restrictions on their European squad by the end of last week and had issued a 'take it or leave it' offer over the weekend.` — [Manchester City's financial fair play deal is still on the rocks, The Guardian, 2014](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/may/13/manchester-city-financial-fair-play-deal)
