# Background

- On 28 June 2018, Derby County sold Pride Park Stadium to Gellaw Newco 202 Limited, a company ultimately owned and controlled by club owner and chairman Mel Morris, for £81.1m. The club immediately leased the stadium back, and the transaction generated a substantial profit that materially improved Derby's Profitability and Sustainability position.
    
    - `The sale of Pride Park to Gellaw at a price of £81.1m was completed on 28 June 2018.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 3 July 2018, after reviewing Derby's P&S submission and supporting material, the EFL confirmed that the club had fulfilled its P&S requirement for the 2017/18 reporting period. The EFL used a £74.4m valuation for the stadium at that stage, while leaving open the question of whether the higher £81.1m figure could be used subsequently.
    
    - `Following our review we can confirm that the Club has fulfilled the P&S Requirement.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 18 February 2019, the EFL accepted Derby's request to use the £81.1m sale price in its 2017/18 P&S calculation. The decision followed Derby's submission of further information supporting the valuation.
    
    - `We accept that the 2017/18 P&S Result can be adjusted to reflect the £81.1m sales price.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 15 April 2019, the EFL continued its review of Derby's 2019 P&S submission. The initial queries did not concern the sale or valuation of Pride Park, although further correspondence during April, May and June later brought the stadium valuation back into the review.
    
    - `Further queries – including what the parties described as the “stadium valuation question” – continued to be addressed in correspondence during April, May and June 2019.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 24 May 2019, Middlesbrough FC's solicitors sent a Letter Before Claim concerning Derby's use of the Pride Park sale in its P&S calculations. Middlesbrough argued that the stadium profits should not have been included and also challenged whether the £81.1m sale represented Fair Market Value.
    
    - `MFC complained that the inclusion of such profits had caused the Club’s P&S Submissions to satisfy the P&S Requirements.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 9 July 2019, Middlesbrough's solicitors argued that Pride Park had not been sold at Fair Market Value and proposed a value of £22,794,520, approximately £58.3m below the £81.1m transaction price.
    
    - `a sale price of £22,794,520 ... was “more realistic”` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 11 July 2019, the EFL told Derby that its forecast three-year Adjusted Earnings Before Tax figure was around £37.1m and therefore within the Upper Loss Threshold, but warned that its review of the 2017/18 and 2018/19 figures was continuing. The EFL lifted the financial embargo that had been imposed after Derby's delayed P&S submission, while reserving the right to raise further queries.
    
    - `There is an ongoing review of the Club’s 2017/18 actual financial results against its P&S Calculation.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 16 August 2019, the EFL told Middlesbrough that it had commissioned an independent valuation of Pride Park and would consider whether the value used in Derby's P&S returns was a Fair Market Value under the regulations.
    
    - `an independent valuation of Pride Park had been commissioned, but not yet received` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 6 September 2019, Middlesbrough served a Notice of Arbitration on the EFL after continuing to challenge the league's handling of Derby's stadium sale and the associated P&S issues.
    
    - `On 6 September 2019 MFC served Notice of Arbitration on the EFL.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- In September 2019, the EFL's appointed valuers, WHE, raised questions about the 2018 JLL valuation of Pride Park and requested further information concerning the assumptions used in the valuation. Derby supplied information to the EFL, while the EFL sought direct answers from JLL.
    
    - `WHE raised certain queries and requested certain information` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 6 December 2019, Derby's solicitors declined to make substantive submissions on the WHE report, arguing that the EFL had already accepted the stadium valuation and had no power to reopen the matter. Derby relied on the earlier EFL communications and approvals concerning the Pride Park transaction.
    
    - `The EFL has already exercised its powers under P&S Rule 2.3 and approved the FMV.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        

# The Charges

- On 6 January 2020, the EFL Executive determined that the Fair Market Value of Pride Park at the time of the 2018 sale was £50m rather than the £81.1m recorded in Derby's accounts. The EFL ordered that the relevant P&S calculations be restated using £50m, which on the EFL's calculation would put Derby above the Upper Loss Threshold by more than £13m for the three-year period ending 30 June 2018 and by more than £26m for the period ending 30 June 2019.
    
    - `The Executive has formally determined that the Fair Market Value to be attributed to the sale of Pride Park should be restated to £50m.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 16 January 2020, the EFL formally charged Derby County with two breaches of the Championship Profitability and Sustainability Rules. The first concerned the valuation of Pride Park and the effect of the EFL's £50m restatement on Derby's losses. The second concerned Derby's accounting treatment of player registrations in its 2015/16, 2016/17 and 2017/18 accounts.
    
    - `The Football League Limited (‘the EFL’) brings 2 charges against Derby County Football Club Limited.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 17 January 2020, Derby publicly rejected both charges and said it would vigorously contest them. The club argued that the EFL had previously approved the stadium arrangements and valuation submissions and had never raised the player-amortisation issue before issuing the second charge.
    
    - `The Club shall strongly contest the challenge to the valuation of Pride Park stadium, as well as the newly notified charge in respect of intangible fixed asset amortisation.` — [Club Statement: 17th January 2020, Derby County, 2020](https://www.dcfc.co.uk/news/2020/01/club-statement-17th-january-2020)
        
    - `The Club shall argue that the very bringing of the Charges itself is unlawful.` — [Club Statement: 17th January 2020, Derby County, 2020](https://www.dcfc.co.uk/news/2020/01/club-statement-17th-january-2020)
        
- On 31 January 2020, reporting revealed that football-finance academic Kieran Maguire had warned the EFL in June 2018 about Derby's player-registration accounting policy. The policy used expected residual or resale values in calculating amortisation, reducing annual amortisation charges compared with a conventional straight-line approach.
    
    - `the EFL was warned about Derby County's unusual player registration model` — [Derby County: EFL was warned about unusual player registration model, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/51322797)
        
    - `using “residual value” is not in line with generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)` — [Derby County: EFL was warned about unusual player registration model, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/51322797)
        
- On 14-21 July 2020, the Independent Disciplinary Commission heard the two charges over five days by remote hearing. The Commission considered more than 7,000 pages of documents together with written submissions and expert evidence.
    
    - `We heard evidence and submissions on the First and Second Charges over a 5 day period between 14 July and 21 July 2020.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 24 August 2020, the Independent Disciplinary Commission dismissed the first charge concerning Pride Park. It found that the EFL had not established the case necessary to prove that the £81.1m consideration should be replaced by the lower figure for the purposes of the P&S rules.
    
    - `The First Charge is dismissed.` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
- On 24 August 2020, the Commission also rejected four of the five particulars advanced under the second charge, while the first particular had already been withdrawn by the EFL. The Commission found the fifth particular proven because Derby had failed adequately to disclose the nature and/or effect of changes to its amortisation policy in the 2016, 2017 and 2018 accounts.
    
    - `the fifth Particular of the Second Charge is proven` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        
    - `the Club’s annual financial statements ... failed to adequately disclose those changes to its accounting policies and/or estimates` — [EFL v Derby County, Disciplinary Commission Decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/c9fc5dceaa7f4b62b81dca0b9e2f7c9d/efl-v-derby-county--decision.pdf)
        

# Appeal

- On 7 September 2020, the EFL appealed the dismissal of the second, third and fourth particulars of the second charge. The stadium charge was not appealed.
    
    - `The EFL appealed against the rejection of the remainder of the second charge` — [EFL v Derby County FC Appeal, League Arbitration Panel, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/efl-v-derby-county---appeal-decision.pdf)
        
- On 3 December 2020, the League Arbitration Panel rejected the EFL's application for the appeal to be heard as a de novo hearing. On 22 January 2021, it also rejected the EFL's application to introduce fresh evidence that had not been before the original Disciplinary Commission.
    
    - `We rejected [the EFL's application]` — [EFL v Derby County FC Appeal, League Arbitration Panel, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/efl-v-derby-county---appeal-decision.pdf)
        
- On 20-21 March 2021, the League Arbitration Panel heard the substantive appeal. The appeal concerned whether Derby's amortisation policy could take possible future resale values of players into account under the FRS 102 cost model.
    
    - `it was impermissible under the cost model for the Club to take into account possible resale values of players` — [EFL v Derby County FC Appeal, League Arbitration Panel, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/efl-v-derby-county---appeal-decision.pdf)
        
- On 7 May 2021, the League Arbitration Panel allowed the EFL's appeal on the second particular of the second charge, finding that Derby's use of possible resale values was impermissible under the cost model. It dismissed the EFL's appeal against the third and fourth particulars. The original finding on the fifth particular, concerning inadequate disclosure, remained in place.
    
    - `We allow the appeal in relation to the second particular and dismiss it in relation to the third and fourth particulars.` — [EFL v Derby County FC Appeal, League Arbitration Panel, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/efl-v-derby-county---appeal-decision.pdf)
        
    - `the charge is proved on the second particular of the second charge` — [EFL v Derby County FC Appeal, League Arbitration Panel, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/efl-v-derby-county---appeal-decision.pdf)
        

# Aftermath

- On 18 June 2021, the original Disciplinary Commission reconvened to determine the sanction arising from the two proven breaches of the second charge. The parties agreed that the sanction question should be remitted to the Commission after the League Arbitration Panel's ruling.
    
    - `The hearing to determine sanction took place on Friday 18 June 2021.` — [EFL v Derby County, Decision and Written Reasons on Sanction, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/210630---efl-v-derby-county---decision-on-sanction-final.pdf)
        
- On 23 June 2021, the Disciplinary Commission delivered its sanction decision. It ordered Derby to restate its 2015/16, 2016/17 and 2017/18 annual accounts using a compliant amortisation policy and issued a reprimand and £100,000 fine. The Commission did not impose a points deduction.
    
    - `We do not impose any deduction of points or other sporting sanction.` — [EFL v Derby County, Decision and Written Reasons on Sanction, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/210630---efl-v-derby-county---decision-on-sanction-final.pdf)
        
    - `The Club is reprimanded and warned as to its future conduct` — [EFL v Derby County, Decision and Written Reasons on Sanction, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/210630---efl-v-derby-county---decision-on-sanction-final.pdf)
        
    - `The Club shall pay a financial penalty of £100,000` — [EFL v Derby County, Decision and Written Reasons on Sanction, EFL, 2021](https://www.efl.com/contentassets/873a8914e09740d3b3a8848131ea10b8/210630---efl-v-derby-county---decision-on-sanction-final.pdf)
        
- On 2 July 2021, the EFL confirmed that it would not appeal the £100,000 fine, reprimand or the associated sanction decision. The EFL stated that Derby's restated accounts would be prepared using a compliant amortisation method and that the league would then reassess whether the revised figures showed any P&S breach.
    
    - `there are insufficient grounds to appeal the sanction imposed by the Independent Disciplinary Commission.` — [EFL Statement: Derby County Sanction (Written Reasons), Derby County, 2021](https://www.dcfc.co.uk/news/2021/07/efl-statement-derby-county-sanction-written-reasons)
        
- On 16 November 2021, after the outstanding P&S issues had been resolved through an Agreed Decision, Derby accepted a further nine-point sporting sanction with three additional points suspended for breaches of the League's P&S rules. The agreement also brought the historic financial proceedings to a conclusion, although the nine-point sanction was imposed in the separate 2021/22 P&S process rather than as the direct sanction imposed in June 2021 for the two original January 2020 charges.
    
    - `The EFL has today confirmed that Derby County Football Club has received a nine-point sporting sanction with a further three points suspended after admitting to breaches of the League’s Profitability and Sustainability (P&S) rules.` — [EFL Statement: Derby County, 16th November 2021, Derby County, 2021](https://www.dcfc.co.uk/news/2021/11/efl-statement-derby-county-16th-november-2021)
