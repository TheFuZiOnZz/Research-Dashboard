The clubs subsequently dropped their threat of legal action after discussions with the Premier League, which indicated that Everton had complied with the applicable rules at that stage.
    
    - `Leeds and Burnley have dropped the threat of legal action against Everton and the Premier League over the Toffees' finances.`

# The Charge

- On 24 March 2023, the Premier League formally referred Everton to an independent Commission for an alleged breach of its Profitability and Sustainability Rules. The assessment period specified by the Premier League was the period ending with the 2021/22 season.
    
    - `In accordance with Premier League Rule W.82.1, the Premier League confirms that it has today referred an alleged breach of the League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules by Everton Football Club to a Commission under Premier League Rule W.3.4.`
        
        `The assessment period for which it is alleged that the Club is in breach is the period ending Season 2021/22.` — [Premier League statement, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3120469) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3120469?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Premier League statement"))
        
- On 24 March 2023, the Premier League confirmed that the case would be heard by an independent Commission appointed through the Premier League's Judicial Panel. The proceedings were to be confidential and conducted in private, with the Commission's final award to be published.
    
    - `Commissions are independent of the Premier League and member clubs.`
        
        `The proceedings before the Commission will, in accordance with Premier League Rule W.82, be confidential and heard in private. Under Premier League Rule W.82.2, the Commission’s final award will be published on the Premier League’s website.` — [Premier League statement, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3120469) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3120469?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Premier League statement"))
        
- On 31 March 2023, Everton publicly denied wrongdoing following the referral and stated that it was prepared to defend its position. The club's 2021/22 accounts, published shortly afterwards, showed a £44.7m loss.
    
    - `Everton denied wrongdoing after the Premier League's referral and said they were "prepared to robustly defend" their position.` — [Premier League: Everton & Newcastle announce financial losses for 2021-22, BBC Sport, 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/65143377) ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/65143377?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Premier League: Everton & Newcastle announce financial losses for 2021-22 - BBC Sport"))
        
- On 15 June 2023, the date of the Commission hearing was confirmed as 25 October 2023. The precise allegations had not been publicly disclosed by the Premier League at that point. Reporting indicated that the case concerned the treatment of interest and other costs associated with Everton's new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock, although Everton continued to deny wrongdoing.
    
    - `Everton's alleged breach of the Premier League's financial fair play rules will go before an independent commission on Wednesday, 25 October.`

# The Commission

- On 25 October 2023, the independent Commission began its hearing into Everton's alleged breach. During the proceedings, Everton admitted that it had breached the PSRs for the period ending 2021/22, although the extent of the breach remained disputed.
    
    - `During the proceedings, the Club admitted it was in breach of the PSRs for the period ending Season 2021/22 but the extent of the breach remained in dispute.` — [Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission"))
        
- On 17 November 2023, the independent Commission published its decision and found that Everton's PSR calculation for the relevant period produced a loss of £124.5m. This exceeded the permitted £105m threshold by £19.5m.
    
    - `Following a five-day hearing last month, the Commission determined that Everton FC’s PSR Calculation for the relevant period resulted in a loss of £124.5million, as contended by the Premier League, which exceeded the threshold of £105million permitted under the PSRs.` — [Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission"))
        
- On 17 November 2023, the Commission imposed an immediate 10-point deduction on Everton. The sanction followed its finding that the club had exceeded the PSR threshold by £19.5m.
    
    - `An independent Commission has imposed an immediate deduction of 10 points on Everton FC for a breach of the Premier League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSRs).`
        
        `The Commission concluded that a sporting sanction in the form of a 10-point deduction should be imposed. That sanction has immediate effect.` — [Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3788486?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton FC deducted 10 points by independent Commission"))
        
- On 17 November 2023, the Commission's published reasoning identified Everton's overspending, particularly on players, its inability to generate expected player-sale income and its lower-than-projected league finish as significant factors in the club's financial position. The Commission noted that Everton's 16th-place finish in 2021/22 had resulted in approximately £21m of lost expected income.
    
    - `The cause of the club's issues was because of overspending - largely on new players - along with an inability to sell players, and a lower than projected league finish.`
        
        `The club's 16th-place finish in 2021-22 caused a loss of expected income of around £21m.` — [Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach, BBC Sport, 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714) ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach - BBC Sport"))
        
- On 17 November 2023, the Commission considered Everton's arguments concerning the COVID-19 pandemic, the loss of commercial arrangements connected to Alisher Usmanov, the Russia-Ukraine war and stadium-related interest costs. The Commission did not accept these matters as sufficient to remove the breach, while it did give some weight to the improving trend in Everton's PSR figures.
    
    - `Further mitigating factors submitted by Everton surrounded the Covid-19 pandemic and the war in Ukraine.`
        
        `Similarly, the commission found that both the loss of a naming rights agreement with Russian billionaire Alisher Usmanov's company USM Services Limited and a rise in stadium-related costs following Russia's invasion of Ukraine could be considered "the type of event that businesses experience".` — [Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach, BBC Sport, 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714) ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach - BBC Sport"))
        
- On 17 November 2023, Everton stated that it was shocked and disappointed by the Commission's decision and disputed aspects of the reasoning and severity of the sanction. The club announced that it would appeal the decision.
    
    - `The club said they were "both shocked and disappointed" and would appeal.` — [Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach, BBC Sport, 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714) ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/67448714?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton receive immediate 10-point Premier League deduction for financial rules breach - BBC Sport"))
        

# Appeal

- On 1 December 2023, Everton formally appealed the 10-point deduction. The club admitted the PSR breach itself but challenged the sanction imposed by the independent Commission.
    
    - `Everton Football Club has appealed the decision of an independent Commission to impose a 10-point deduction on the club, after its admission of a breach of the Premier League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules.` — [Everton FC appeal decision of independent Commission, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3802547) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3802547?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton FC appeal decision of independent Commission"))
        
- On 1 December 2023, Everton's appeal was lodged with the Chair of the Premier League Judicial Panel, who was responsible for appointing an independent Appeal Board to hear the case.
    
    - `The club lodged the appeal to the Chair of the Judicial Panel today, who will now appoint an Appeal Board to hear the case.` — [Everton FC appeal decision of independent Commission, Premier League, 2023](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3802547) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3802547?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Everton FC appeal decision of independent Commission"))
        
- On 26 February 2024, the independent Appeal Board reduced Everton's original 10-point deduction to six points. Everton had appealed on nine grounds, all concerning the sanction rather than the fact of the breach; two of those grounds were upheld.
    
    - `An independent Appeal Board has concluded that the sanction for Everton FC’s breach of the Premier League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSRs), for the period ending Season 2021/22, will be an immediate six-point deduction.`
        
        `Everton FC appealed the sanction imposed against it on nine grounds, each of which related to the sanction rather than the fact of the breach, which the club admitted.`
        
        `Two of those nine grounds were upheld by the Appeal Board, which has substituted the original points deduction of 10 for six.` — [Premier League statement, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3912574) ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3912574?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Premier League statement"))
