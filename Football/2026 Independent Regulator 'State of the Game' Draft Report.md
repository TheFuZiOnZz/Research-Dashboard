# Background
- On 21 July 2025, the Football Governance Act established three core statutory objectives for the Independent Football Regulator: protecting the financial soundness of regulated clubs, promoting the financial resilience of English football and safeguarding the heritage of English football.
    - `Protect and promote the financial soundness of regulated football clubs`
      
      `Protect and promote financial resilience of English football`
      
      `Safeguard the heritage of English football` - [About Us, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://www.footballregulator.org.uk/about-us)
- In 2025, the IFR began developing a statutory regulatory framework for clubs across the English professional football pyramid. Its powers include introducing a club licensing system, strengthening owners' and directors' tests, monitoring compliance with financial requirements and enforcing standards for supporter engagement and club heritage.
    - The regulator's remit includes protections concerning stadium sales, club badges and home kit colours, alongside powers to prevent clubs from joining certain closed or breakaway competitions that threaten the sustainability and heritage of English football.
    - The IFR also has backstop powers to intervene in financial distribution between leagues if football's governing bodies fail to reach an acceptable agreement.
    - [Information Gathering and Enforcement, Independent Football Regulator](https://engage.footballregulator.org.uk/information-gathering-and-enforcement)
- On 20 January 2026, the IFR announced the scope of its first _State of the Game_ report, an in-depth assessment of the financial condition and governance of English men's professional football. The review covered broadcast revenues, player wages, club ownership, debt, financial distributions, academy development, supporter engagement and club heritage.
    - `For the first time, we will shine a light on the financial pressures, governance gaps, and structural risks facing the football pyramid.` - [IFR sets out scope of groundbreaking State of the Game report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://www.footballregulator.org.uk/posts/ifr-sets-out-scope-of-groundbreaking-state-of-the-game-report)
        

# The Report
- On 13 October 2026, the Independent Football Regulator (IFR) published its first draft State of the Game report, a comprehensive assessment of the financial condition of English men's professional football across the Premier League, Championship, League One, League Two and National League. The report examined revenue growth, club profitability, wages, ownership, debt, financial distributions, supporter engagement and governance.
    - `This is the most comprehensive financial analysis of the English football industry ever undertaken: a painstaking account of the contradictions which underpin our national game. It will demonstrate how it is possible for English club football to simultaneously be the richest, best and most deeply competitive in the world while also standing on the edge of financial disaster.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
    - `The report is intended to provide transparency and an evidence-based, objective assessment of the issues facing the game for the benefit of all stakeholders throughout the pyramid.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that English football generated approximately £9 billion in revenue during 2025/26, but that substantial financial growth had not translated into widespread profitability or financial stability across the football pyramid.
    - `Investors from around the world are queuing up to be part of this international success story with 78 transactions worth more than £7 billion in the past five years. It is our job as the regulator to ensure English football continues to grow, attract investment and maintain its leading position in the global market.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
    - `And yet what this comprehensive report reveals is an underlying fragility: a game loaded with risk that can no longer be ignored.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)

# Revenue Growth and Financial Inequality

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that Premier League revenues had grown dramatically since the competition's formation in 1992, while financial disparities between the divisions and between clubs within the Premier League had widened.
    
    - `Since 1992/93, average Premier League club revenues have grown over 30 times their 1992 level. By comparison, UK GDP only increased by 3 times over the same period.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
    - `The highest earning Premier League club now earns 605 times the revenue of the lowest earning National League club.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that the Premier League accounted for more than 80% of revenues across the top five English divisions in 2025/26, with the six largest Premier League clubs accounting for 56% of Premier League clubs' revenues.
    
    - `However, the lower leagues have not been able to keep up. Gaps between the leagues have grown. Even within the Premier League, the big six have started to pull away.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR identified the unequal distribution of football's growing revenues as a major contributor to financial instability, with clubs spending increasingly large sums to compete with wealthier rivals and pursue promotion.
    
    - `But it is not necessarily the increase in revenues which has created the issues. It is how the money is distributed and spent.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Wages and Operating Costs

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that average player wage costs increased across every division between 2021/22 and 2025/26, with the largest proportional increases occurring in League One and League Two.
    
    - Premier League: player wage costs increased by 33%.
        
    - Championship: player wage costs increased by 32%.
        
    - League One: player wage costs increased by 71%.
        
    - League Two: player wage costs increased by 63%.
        
    - `The very competitiveness which has made English football such an economic powerhouse is also the driver of the surge in player-related costs which leaves the vast majority of clubs unprofitable.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that 17 clubs across the EFL and National League incurred non-player operating costs exceeding their total revenues, meaning they would still have made losses even if they had paid no player wages.
    
    - `But it’s not just player-related costs. This report found that 17 clubs across the EFL and National League incurred non-player operational costs exceeding revenues. This means these clubs would be loss-making even if their players played for free.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Club Losses and Profitability

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that 94% of clubs across the top five English leagues made losses in 2025/26, with average losses increasing substantially across the football pyramid.
    
    - `Faced with such cost pressures, 94% of clubs in the top five English leagues made losses in 2025/26.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that average annual losses increased across the divisions between 2021/22 and 2025/26.
    
    - Premier League: average losses increased from £20 million to £44 million.
        
    - Championship: average losses increased from £11 million to £22 million.
        
    - League One: average losses increased from £2.4 million to £7.5 million.
        
    - League Two: average losses increased from £0.6 million to £2.7 million.
        
    - The IFR reported that average losses in 2025/26 represented approximately 59% of Championship revenues, more than 65% of League One revenues and more than 40% of League Two revenues.
        
    - `Rising costs mean that clubs are increasingly loss-making and reliant on owner-funding.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Owner Funding and Financial Distress

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that most English football clubs relied on external financing, including owner injections, loans and other funding arrangements, to sustain their operations despite persistent losses.
    
    - `This has left nearly all clubs reliant on owner injections or debt to be able to continue trading.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that many clubs depended on frequent injections of money from their owners, leaving them vulnerable if that funding was withdrawn.
    
    - Around two-thirds of clubs reported that they could not survive for more than 90 days without new owner funding.
        
    - 29% of clubs reported that they could not survive for more than 30 days without new owner funding.
        
    - Between March 2025 and March 2026, 67 clubs received six or more owner funding injections, while 28 received at least 12.
        
    - `Around two thirds of clubs reported they would not be able to survive for more than 90 days without new injections of owner funding. More than a quarter said they would not be able to see out the month.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that a majority of EFL and National League clubs did not have contingency plans for situations in which their owners became unwilling or unable to continue providing financial support.
    
    - `The vast majority of EFL and National League clubs have significantly negative operating cashflow, meaning that they burn cash as a result of day-to-day operations.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Debt and Technical Insolvency

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that more than 60% of EFL and National League clubs had liabilities exceeding the book value of their assets, meaning they were technically insolvent on an accounting basis. The report also found that approximately 20% of Premier League clubs were in the same position.
    
    - `60% of EFL and National League clubs have liabilities that exceed the book value of their assets meaning they are technically insolvent.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that 27% of EFL and National League clubs had combined financial and transfer debt exceeding the estimated saleable value of their businesses.
    
    - The report noted that accounting for the estimated market value of playing squads improved the financial position of some clubs, although 46% of EFL clubs would still have negative equity.
        

# Transfer Debt and Agent Fees

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that transfer costs across English football had risen sharply between 2021/22 and 2025/26, with annual player-registration amortisation doubling from £1.3 billion to £2.6 billion.
    
    - Total agent fees increased from £317 million to £484 million over the same period, representing 16% of English clubs' total transfer expenditure in 2025/26.
        
    - English clubs reported £4.8 billion in transfer payables and £1.5 billion in transfer receivables in 2025/26, leaving net transfer debt of approximately £3.4 billion.
        
    - The IFR identified transfer factoring as one of the reasons for the imbalance between transfer payables and receivables.
        
    - [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Revenue Distribution and Parachute Payments

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR identified financial disparities between leagues as a major source of instability, particularly the financial consequences of relegation from the Premier League and the incentives for Championship clubs to spend heavily in pursuit of promotion.
    
    - `The existing system of parachute payments contributes to the incentives for Championship clubs to overspend, driving up wage inflation and placing even greater risks on those teams chasing the dream of a place in the Premier League.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that Premier League parachute payments totalled £226 million in 2025/26, compared with £138 million in solidarity payments to EFL clubs.
    
    - The report stated that parachute payments and solidarity distributions had both increased since 2007/08, but parachute payments had grown more and were concentrated among fewer clubs.
        
    - The Premier League distributed £3.1 billion to its own clubs in 2025/26, alongside £226 million in parachute payments, £141 million in solidarity payments to EFL clubs and £5 million to National League clubs.
        
    - [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR called for reforms to revenue distribution arrangements to reduce financial cliff-edges between divisions and address incentives that encouraged clubs to take excessive financial risks.
    
    - The report identified the need to mitigate the financial impact of relegation from the Premier League and reduce the distortive effects of parachute payments on Championship clubs' financial incentives.
        
    - The IFR also warned that changes to distribution arrangements should not trigger further increases in clubs' spending.
        
    - [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Fan Engagement and Club Heritage

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that formal fan engagement practices were widespread in the top four divisions but less consistent in the National League. The report identified supporter engagement as an important part of club governance and decision-making.
    
    - `Overall, clubs, supporter groups and fans engage for a range of different reasons, but common themes emerge. Fan engagement is valued for strengthening club-supporter relationships, improving supporter experience, incorporating fans’ views and supporting better-informed decision-making.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR reported that supporters placed particular importance on having a say over ticket prices and allocations, the matchday experience and their clubs' approach to fan engagement.
    
    - Around 40% of surveyed supporters believed fans should have a say in changes to club heritage, including names, crests and colours, while 38% believed fans should have a say in changes to home stadiums.
        
    - The IFR proposed stronger expectations for clubs to publish fan engagement plans and explain how supporter feedback had been addressed.
        
    - [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
        

# Proposed Reforms

- On 13 October 2026, the IFR set out six areas for action intended to improve the financial sustainability and governance of English football.
    
    - Reform revenue distribution to improve financial stability, mitigate the effects of relegation and reduce the distortive impact of parachute payments.
        
    - Encourage leagues, clubs and other stakeholders to take action to reduce financial losses.
        
    - Address financial and governance risks at National League level.
        
    - Require clubs to improve financial risk management and prepare action plans to demonstrate how they will meet the statutory Appropriate Financial Resources requirement by 2030.
        
    - Strengthen clubs' obligations to demonstrate how supporters' views are considered in decision-making.
        
    - Require progress on diversity in club boardrooms and senior executive positions.
        
    - [Independent Football Regulator releases six-point plan to improve clubs’ financial sustainability, The Independent, 2026](https://www.independent.co.uk/bulletin/sport/independent-football-regulator-state-of-game-report-b3066106.html)
        
- On 13 October 2026, the IFR stated that it would prefer football to agree its own solutions to the financial problems identified in the report, but warned that it could use its statutory powers if the industry failed to make sufficient progress.
    
    - `We have always stressed that it would be better if football came to its own agreement which would go some of the way to addressing the issues identified in this report.`
        
    - `If football can’t find a solution we will consider our next steps and will not hesitate to use all the powers at our disposal to bring about lasting change.` — [State of the Game Report, Independent Football Regulator, 2026](https://sotg.footballregulator.org.uk/api/media/file/IFR%20State%20of%20the%20Game%20Report.pdf?from=header)
