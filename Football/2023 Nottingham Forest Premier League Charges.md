# Background

- On 29 May 2022, Nottingham Forest secured promotion to the Premier League by defeating Huddersfield Town 1–0 in the Championship play-off final at Wembley. Forest had spent the previous two seasons in the Championship, a fact that would later affect the club's applicable Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR) threshold.
    - `Nottingham Forest secured promotion to the Premier League for the first time in 23 years with a 1-0 win over Huddersfield Town in the Championship play-off final.` — [Nottingham Forest promoted to Premier League after 1-0 play-off final win over Huddersfield, BBC Sport, 2022](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/61600792)
- During the 2022 summer transfer window, Forest made a large number of signings following promotion. The club ultimately signed more than 20 players during the season as it rebuilt its squad for Premier League football.
    - `Nottingham Forest completed the signing of their 22nd player of the summer transfer window when they brought in Willy Boly from Wolves.` — [Nottingham Forest: Premier League new boys complete record-breaking transfer window, BBC Sport, 2022](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/62738418)
- On 31 March 2023, Forest submitted a projected PSR calculation to the Premier League for the financial year ending 30 June 2023. The club was already aware that the applicable PSR threshold was lower than the standard £105m because it had spent two seasons in the Championship during the relevant assessment period.
    - `Due to Nottingham Forest spending two seasons in the Championship within the three-year assessment period, the maximum loss for the club is £61million.` — [Everton and Nottingham Forest confirm Premier League PSR breaches, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3858986)
- On 28 May 2023, Forest completed their first Premier League season since promotion by finishing 16th, four points above the relegation zone. Their 38 points ultimately left them in the Premier League for a second consecutive season.
    - `Nottingham Forest finished 16th in the Premier League in 2022/23, recording nine wins, 11 draws and 18 defeats for a total of 38 points.` — [Nottingham Forest Football Club Season History, Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/clubs/15/Nottm-Forest/season-history)
- On 1 September 2023, Forest sold Brennan Johnson to Tottenham Hotspur for £47.5m. The transfer was completed after the 30 June accounting deadline and therefore did not count towards Forest's PSR calculation for the assessment period that had already ended.
    - `Tottenham have agreed a fee in excess of £45m to sign Brennan Johnson from Nottingham Forest on transfer deadline day.` - [Brennan Johnson: Tottenham agree £45m-plus deadline day deal with Nottingham Forest, BBC Sport, 2023](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/66686668)
- On 31 December 2023, Forest submitted its PSR report to the Premier League. The calculation showed that the club's losses exceeded the applicable £61m threshold.
    - `The Premier League’s case is that Forest’s PSR Calculation, dated 31 December 2023, demonstrates losses in excess of the £61m threshold applicable to Forest, as a newly promoted club. The Premier League considers that Forest’s PSR Calculation results in a loss of £95,536,000, breaching the £61m threshold by £34,536,000.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)

# The Commission

- On 15 January 2024, the Premier League confirmed that Nottingham Forest had admitted breaching its Profitability and Sustainability Rules for the assessment period ending with the 2022/23 season. The club's PSR calculation exceeded its applicable threshold by £34.5m.
    - `The two clubs have each confirmed to the Premier League that they are in breach of the League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules`
      
      `Everton FC and Nottingham Forest FC have each confirmed to the Premier League that they are in breach of the League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR). This is as a result of sustaining losses above the permitted thresholds for the assessment period ending Season 2022/23.`
      
      `In accordance with Premier League Rules, both cases have now been referred to the chair of the Judicial Panel, who will appoint separate Commissions to determine the appropriate sanction.`
      
      `Commissions are independent of the Premier League and member clubs. The proceedings are heard in private with the Commissions’ final decisions made public on the Premier League’s website. The League will make no further comment until that time.` — [Everton and Nottingham Forest confirm Premier League PSR breaches, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3858986)
    - `A Club will be in breach of the PSRs if its PSR Calculation over the relevant period results in a loss in excess of £105 million (with that threshold reduced by £22million for each season that the relevant Club has been in the Championship during the relevant period)`
      
      `Due to Nottingham Forest spending two seasons in the Championship within the three-year assessment period, the maximum loss for the club is £61million.` — [Everton and Nottingham Forest confirm Premier League PSR breaches, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3858986)
- On 7 and 8 March 2024, the independent Commission held its hearing into Forest's PSR breach. 
- On 18 March 2024, the independent Commission imposed an immediate four-point deduction on Nottingham Forest for the PSR breach relating to the assessment period ending in 2022/23. The Commission found that Forest had exceeded its permitted threshold by £34.5m.
    - `The four points sanction is not to punish Forest so much as it is to be fair to the other clubs; to give the public confidence that when a club invests as Forest did to compete in the Premier League it still needs to comply with the PSR Threshold for losses; and that when a club like Forest took the risk of effectively ignoring the PSR warning from its Finance Director before the January window in 2023, and rather than looking to sell players, it added players to its squad, ultimately leaving itself with just two weeks to sell Player A in the summer 2023 window, such risk taking and “sailing close to the wind” needs a proportionate sanction to maintain the integrity of the Premier League; and to hopefully enable Forest to operate a more sustainable business in terms of PSR going forwards. Suspending this sanction would not change anything. If the Commission’s aim was to hold over some punishment to give Forest a chance to change its ways, then perhaps a suspension could be considered, but here Forest appears to understand its PSR requirements for this current season and what it needs to do to avoid being in a similar situation in a year’s time. It has already told the Commission of its positive trend and that it will not have PSR issues at the end of FY2024. The Commission hopes that will be the case. The four points are to be deducted immediately` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)
- The Premier League's case was that Forest's PSR calculation produced an adjusted loss of £95.536m, £34.536m above the £61m permitted threshold.
    - `By a complaint dated 15 January 2024, the Premier League Board (the "Premier League" or the "PL") alleges that Nottingham Forest Football Club Limited ("Forest" or the "Club") has acted in breach of the Premier League’s Profitability & Sustainability Rules (the "PSR").`
        
        `The Premier League’s case is that Forest’s PSR Calculation, dated 31 December 2023, demonstrates losses in excess of the £61m threshold applicable to Forest, as a newly promoted club. The Premier League considers that Forest’s PSR Calculation results in a loss of £95,536,000, breaching the £61m threshold by £34,536,000.
        
        `Forest accepts that it is in breach of the PSR but asserts that this ought to be considered in light of certain exceptional circumstances which the Commission has considered below.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)
- During the Commission proceedings, Forest argued that the timing of Brennan Johnson's eventual sale should be treated as a significant mitigating factor. The club argued that it had received an offer from Atlético Madrid on 30 June and subsequently sold Johnson to Tottenham for £47.5m on 1 September, meaning that the club had ultimately generated a substantial profit from the player's sale, but after the relevant accounting deadline.
    - `The club knew they would need to sell a player to comply with PSR by 30 June and described their failure to do so as a "near miss". They received an offer for Johnson of €50m (£42.9m) on that date from Atlético Madrid but demanded €65m (£55.8m).` — [Nottingham Forest docked four points for Premier League financial rules breach, The Guardian, 2024](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2024/mar/18/nottingham-forest-docked-four-points-premier-league-financial-rules-breach-profitability-and-sustainability)
- Forest also argued that its circumstances were unusual because its PSR threshold had been reduced from the standard £105m to £61m as a consequence of having spent two seasons in the Championship. The club argued that this placed it in a different financial position from established Premier League clubs.
    - `Forest claimed that it faced a lower PSR Threshold than other clubs, having been in the EFL the season prior to the breach, but it had not had the benefit of Parachute Payments whilst in the EFL, as the other two promoted clubs had.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Decision on Costs, Premier League, 2025](https://resources.premierleague.pulselive.com/premierleague/document/2025/06/09/e3f28ff3-c998-4a42-812d-186cd35723ec/PL-v-NFFC-Decision-on-Costs.pdf)
- The Commission acknowledged that Forest had demonstrated what it described as “exceptional cooperation” throughout the investigation and proceedings. However, it did not accept all of Forest's proposed mitigating factors when determining the sanction.
    - `The independent Commission determined the sanction following a two-day hearing this month, at which the club had the opportunity to detail a range of mitigating factors. The Commission found that the club had demonstrated "exceptional cooperation" in its dealings with the Premier League throughout the process.` — [Nottingham Forest FC deducted four points by independent Commission, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3936397)
- The Commission rejected Forest's argument that the eventual sale of Johnson should substantially mitigate the breach. It considered that Forest could have done more to complete a sale before the 30 June deadline and did not regard the two-month gap between the accounting deadline and Johnson's eventual transfer as a “near miss”.
	- `The Premier League contends that the sale of a player outside the relevant financial year cannot constitute mitigation unless the facts are exceptional. Forest relies on the Sheffield Wednesday decision as indicating that a “near miss” can constitute mitigation, but the Premier League points out the Sheffield Wednesday case did not concern the sale of a player and is distinguishable. It also involved a related-party transaction with an entity associated with the club’s owner, and there is no reason to believe that its timing had any effect on its value. Here Forest chose to wait in order to maximise the transfer fee.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)
	- `The Commission notes the actual “near miss” could be that Forest nearly missed selling Player A in the summer window altogether – it was cut extremely fine; however, it is whether Forest’s missing the PSR deadline of 30 June 2023 was near or not that is the issue.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)
    - `The Commission would not see a sale in the January transfer window in 2024 as being a “near miss” at all. It could categorise a sale in the summer 2023 as a “near miss”, if it was truly near to the PSR deadline or at the first available reasonable opportunity proximate to the deadline. Forest here waited until Mr Marinakis gave the sale instruction on 28 August 2023 and managed to sell Player A on the very last day of the summer window.` — [Premier League v Nottingham Forest FC, Premier League Independent Commission, 2024](https://svenhaist.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Die-Urteilsbegruendung-zum-Punktabzug-fuer-Nottingham-Forest.pdf)
- The Commission's decision left Forest with an immediate four-point reduction in its Premier League total. The sanction was imposed during the 2023/24 season under new Premier League procedures intended to ensure PSR cases were resolved within the same season in which the complaint was issued.
    - `The NFFC case proceeded in accordance with the new Standard Directions, set out at Appendix 1 to the Rules.`
        
        `Clubs agreed to introduce Standard Directions for PSR cases ... that seek to ensure such cases are resolved in the same season the complaint is issued, with any sanction handed down prior to the subsequent Annual General Meeting.` — [Nottingham Forest FC deducted four points by independent Commission, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3936397)

# Appeal

- On 25 March 2024, Nottingham Forest formally appealed the independent Commission's four-point deduction. The appeal challenged the Commission's treatment of the Brennan Johnson sale as a mitigating factor and its decision not to suspend some or all of the points deduction.
    
    - `Nottingham Forest Football Club has appealed the decision of an independent Commission to impose a four-point deduction on the club, after its admission of a breach of the Premier League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules.`
        
        `The club lodged the appeal to the Chair of the Judicial Panel today, who will now appoint an Appeal Board to hear the case.` — [Nottingham Forest FC appeal decision of independent Commission, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3941350)
        
- On 24 April 2024, an independent three-person Appeal Board considered Forest's appeal. The Board examined the club's arguments concerning the treatment of Johnson's post-deadline sale and the Commission's decision to impose an immediate rather than suspended points deduction.
    
    - `The club argued that the independent Commission committed an error in not treating its sale of a high-profile player shortly after the assessment period as a mitigating factor, and that it committed a further error in electing not to suspend some or all of the points deduction it imposed.` — [Independent Appeal Board decision on Nottingham Forest, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3999776)
        
- On 7 May 2024, the independent Appeal Board rejected Forest's appeal and upheld the four-point deduction. The Board found that the Commission had been entitled to impose the immediate sanction and rejected both grounds advanced by the club.
    
    - `An independent Appeal Board has upheld the decision of a Commission to deduct four points from Nottingham Forest following an admitted breach of the Premier League’s Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR).`
        
        `Each of these grounds was rejected by the Appeal Board, which found the independent Commission was entitled to immediately impose the sanction it did. The four-point deduction will therefore remain in place.` — [Independent Appeal Board decision on Nottingham Forest, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3999776)
        

# Aftermath

- On 19 May 2024, Forest completed the 2023/24 Premier League season in 17th place with 32 points, three points clear of relegated Luton Town. The four-point deduction was therefore reflected in Forest's final league position and points total.
    
    - `Nottingham Forest finished 17th in the Premier League in 2023/24, recording nine wins, nine draws and 20 defeats for 32 points.` — [Nottingham Forest Football Club Season History, Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/clubs/15/Nottm-Forest/season-history)
        
- The Premier League's official season review recorded that Forest's 32-point total was the lowest tally by a team to avoid relegation in Premier League history.
    
    - `Forest’s 32 points was the lowest-ever tally for a Premier League team to avoid relegation.` — [2023/24 Premier League end of season REVIEW, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4039318)
        
- On 7 May 2024, following the rejection of Forest's appeal, the four-point deduction became final. The Premier League subsequently amended the league table notation to record that Forest had been deducted four points following its PSR breach.
    
    - `The four-point deduction will therefore remain in place.`
        
        `The note attached to the asterisk on the Premier League table will now be amended to read: "Deducted four points following a breach of the Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSRs)".` — [Independent Appeal Board decision on Nottingham Forest, Premier League, 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3999776)
