# Background

- On 31 March 2018, Sheffield Wednesday was required to submit its Profitability and Sustainability documentation to the English Football League (EFL). The club failed to make the required submission and was subsequently placed under a player-registration embargo. At the time, the EFL's Championship rules permitted aggregate losses of up to £39m over a three-year monitoring period.
    
    - `In the season 2017/18, Clubs in the Championship were required under the P&S Rules to submit to the EFL by 31 March 2018 certain documents including a Profit and Sustainability Calculation and the Club's Future Financial Information. The Club did not do this and was consequently placed under a player registration embargo pursuant to` — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        
- On 7 August 2018, Sheffield Wednesday confirmed that it had been under an EFL transfer embargo since April. The club's previous financial figures showed a loss of more than £20m for 2016/17, following a £9.8m loss in 2015/16.
    
    - `Championship side Sheffield Wednesday are under a transfer embargo and have been since April.`
        
        `Under the EFL's profitability and sustainability rules, clubs are not allowed to post losses in excess of £39m over three years without punishment.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Owls confirm they are under transfer embargo, BBC Sport, 2018](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/45098384)
        
- In early August 2018, Sheffield Wednesday and the EFL held discussions concerning the club's financial position. The subsequent independent Commission found that the discussions resulted in an arrangement under which owner Dejphon Chansiri agreed to purchase Hillsborough Stadium from the club for the purpose of ensuring that Wednesday would not breach the P&S Rules for the three-year period ending 31 July 2018.
    
    - `In August 2018 Sheffield Wednesday Football Club (‘the Club’) entered into an arrangement, much debated in these proceedings, whereby the Club’s Chairman, Mr Dejphon Chansiri (‘DC’), agreed to purchase Hillsborough Stadium from the Club with the purpose of ensuring that the Club was not in breach of the Profit and Sustainability (‘P&S’) Rules for the 3-year period ending on 31 July 2018.` — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        
- On 17 August 2018, the EFL lifted Sheffield Wednesday's transfer embargo after the club worked with the League to demonstrate compliance with the P&S regulations.
    
    - `Sheffield Wednesday have had their EFL transfer embargo lifted with immediate effect.`
        
        `The club has worked diligently alongside the EFL in a bid to ensure compliance with Profitability and Sustainability regulations.` — [Sheffield Wednesday transfer embargo lifted, Sky Sports, 2018](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11703/11475256/sheffield-wednesday-transfer-embargo-lifted)
        
- On 28 June 2019, Sheffield Wednesday's newly created company Sheffield 3 Limited acquired Hillsborough Stadium from the football club for £60m. The transaction generated a profit of approximately £38m for the club, which was subsequently included in the accounts for the period ending 31 July 2018.
    
    - `The £60m receipts were included in the 2017-18 accounts whose period had ended on 31 July 2018, 11 months earlier.` — [Three Sheffield Wednesday sponsors linked to owner are not commercially active, The Guardian, 2019](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2019/dec/19/sheffield-wednesday-sponsors-owner-not-commercially-active-dejphon-chansiri)
        
- On 12 July 2019, details of Sheffield Wednesday's published accounts became public. The accounts showed a £38m-plus profit on the sale of Hillsborough, producing a pre-tax profit of £2.5m for 2017/18. Without the stadium transaction, the club would have recorded a pre-tax loss of £35.4m.
    
    - `Sheffield Wednesday Football Club Limited sold the ground for a profit of more than £38m, helping them record a pre-tax profit of £2.5m for 2017-18.`
        
        `Without the sale of the ground, Wednesday would have posted a pre-tax loss of £35.4m.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Owner Dejphon Chansiri buys Hillsborough to ensure club makes profit, BBC Sport, 2019](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/48964055)
        
- On 14 November 2019, the EFL formally charged Sheffield Wednesday with misconduct following its investigation into the Hillsborough transaction. The charges concerned the process by which the stadium was sold, the timing of the transaction and the inclusion of the resulting profit in the 2017/18 accounts.
    
    - `The charges are in respect of a number of allegations regarding the process of how and when the stadium was sold and the inclusion of the profits in the 2017-18 accounts.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: EFL charge Owls with misconduct following Hillsborough sale, BBC Sport, 2019](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/50418776)
        

# The Charges

- On 14 November 2019, the EFL's first charge alleged that Sheffield Wednesday had breached the Profitability and Sustainability Rules by including the profit from the Hillsborough sale in the accounting period ending 31 July 2018. The EFL's position was that the sale should not have been recognised in that accounting period.
    
    - `The EFL formed the view that it was not [valid], that the sale should not have been included within that accounting period and, accordingly, the Club had breached the P&S Rules. Charge 1 is to that effect.` — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        
- The second charge alleged that Sheffield Wednesday had deliberately concealed from the EFL the fact that the Heads of Terms relating to the stadium transaction had been backdated. The allegation was based upon the P&S Rules' requirement that clubs act with the utmost good faith towards the EFL.
    
    - `Charge 2 asserts that the Club “sought to deliberately conceal from the EFL that the Heads of Terms had been backdated.”`
        
        `Each Club shall, at all times and in all matters within the scope of these Rules, behave with the utmost good faith both towards The League and the other Clubs.` — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        
- On 4 December 2019, Sheffield Wednesday rejected the EFL's allegations and argued that the charges were unlawful. The club said it possessed correspondence and other documents which it believed showed that the EFL had previously authorised the transaction and that the club had relied upon that authorisation.
    
    - `The club has in its possession numerous emails, letters and other documents in which the EFL gave authorisation to the transaction, and on which authorisation the club understood it could rely.`
        
        `That authorisation gave rise in law to a legitimate expectation that the transaction would be accepted by the EFL, which is binding on the EFL.` — [Sheffield Wednesday say EFL acting 'unlawfully' over Hillsborough sale charge, BBC Sport, 2019](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/50662764)
        
- On 14 February 2020, the dispute remained unresolved and Sheffield Wednesday continued to challenge the EFL's case. Other Championship clubs were reported to be seeking a substantial points deduction if Wednesday were found guilty.
    
    - `A number of Championship clubs are demanding a serious points deduction be imposed on Sheffield Wednesday, should the club be found guilty of misconduct by an independent disciplinary commission over the sale of its stadium to owner Dejphon Chansiri.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Clubs demand points deduction over sale of Hillsborough to Dejphon Chansiri, Sky Sports, 2020](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11703/11933677/sheffield-wednesday-clubs-demand-points-deduction-over-sale-of-hillsborough-to-dejphon-chansiri)
        
- On 30 March 2020, an initial directions hearing was held virtually. A four-day substantive hearing was subsequently scheduled for 22–25 June, with final submissions on 30 June.
    
    - `Following a “virtual” Directions hearing on 30 March 2020, a 4-day “virtual” hearing was set for 22 - 25 June during which the evidence would be heard, with the final submissions to be heard on 30 June.` — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        
- From 22 to 25 June 2020, the independent Disciplinary Commission heard evidence in the case. The hearing involved evidence from EFL and Sheffield Wednesday officials, auditors, financial experts and owner Dejphon Chansiri. The Commission subsequently heard final submissions on 30 June.
    
    - `We heard oral evidence from the following witnesses:` followed by witnesses including EFL Financial Controller James Karran, EFL Governance and Legal Director Nicholas Craig, former EFL CEO Shaun Harvey, Sheffield Wednesday Financial Director John Redgate, CEO Katrien Meire, auditors and Dejphon Chansiri. — [EFL v Sheffield Wednesday FC, Independent Disciplinary Commission decision, EFL, 2020](https://www.efl.com/siteassets/efl-documents/201920/judgements/efl-v-sheffield-wednesday-fc---decision.pdf)
        

# The Decision

- On 31 July 2020, the independent Disciplinary Commission found Sheffield Wednesday guilty of breaching the EFL's Profitability and Sustainability Rules by including the Hillsborough sale in the wrong accounting period. The Commission imposed a 12-point deduction for the 2020/21 Championship season.
    
    - `An independent disciplinary commission, appointed under EFL Regulations, has ruled that Sheffield Wednesday will receive a 12-point deduction for breaching the League's Profitability and Sustainability Rules for the three season reporting period ending with Season 2017/18.`
        
        `The sporting sanction will take effect in season 2020/21.` — [Independent Disciplinary Commissions and League Arbitration Panels, EFL, 2020](https://efl.com/news/2020/august/independent-disciplinary-commissions-and-league-arbitration-panels/)
        
- The Commission found that Sheffield Wednesday should not have included the profit from the Hillsborough sale in its financial statements for the period ending 31 July 2018. The sale itself was not prohibited by the rules; the issue concerned when the transaction occurred and the accounting treatment applied to it.
    
    - `The club was found guilty based on the fact that the club should not have included profits from the sale of Hillsborough Stadium in the club's financial statements for the period ending July 2018.` — [Sheffield Wednesday deducted 12 points for 2020-21 season, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/53612257)
        
- The Commission dismissed the second charge alleging that Sheffield Wednesday had deliberately concealed information from the EFL. The club was therefore found not guilty of breaching its duty of utmost good faith by deliberately concealing information concerning the stadium transaction.
    
    - `The club was found not guilty of a further charge of breaching its duty of utmost good faith to the EFL by deliberately concealing information from the League in respect of filings made in respect of the Profitability and Sustainability Rules.` — [Independent Disciplinary Commissions and League Arbitration Panels, EFL, 2020](https://efl.com/news/2020/august/independent-disciplinary-commissions-and-league-arbitration-panels/)
        
- The Commission's reasons for applying the deduction to 2020/21 rather than 2019/20 included the substantial delay in proceedings, the additional time taken by the second charge and concerns about the fairness of imposing the deduction after the 2019/20 season had effectively been completed. The EFL had sought to apply the deduction to 2019/20, which would have relegated Wednesday to League One.
    
    - `The EFL wanted to apply Sheffield Wednesday's 12-point deduction for breaching financial rules to the 2019-20 season, which would have relegated the club to League One, but an independent panel prevented it.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: EFL wanted to apply 12-point deduction in 2019-20 season, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/53803735)
        
- The Commission also considered the EFL's handling of the proceedings and the delay caused by the pursuit of the second charge. It concluded that the second charge had substantially extended the proceedings and that the timing of the sanction therefore had to take account of the circumstances in which the case had been heard.
    
    - `It was abundantly plain to us charge 2 was the charge which substantially extended the proceedings.`
        
        `We are quite satisfied that had the commission been required only to deal with charge 1, the substantive hearing could have been dealt with significantly earlier than it was.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: EFL wanted to apply 12-point deduction in 2019-20 season, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/53803735)
        

# Appeal

- On 17 August 2020, the written reasons for the Disciplinary Commission's decision were published. Sheffield Wednesday confirmed that it intended to appeal the 12-point deduction.
    
    - `The South Yorkshire club confirmed after the release of the written reasons that they will appeal.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: EFL wanted to apply 12-point deduction in 2019-20 season, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/53803735)
        
- In August 2020, Sheffield Wednesday lodged an appeal with a League Arbitration Panel against the 12-point sanction. The club challenged both the circumstances surrounding the stadium sale and the severity of the sporting sanction.
    
    - `In August 2020, the club filed an appeal to a League Arbitration Panel against the decision of the EFL Disciplinary Commission to impose a sanction on the club of a deduction of 12 points in season 2020/21.` — [Sheffield Wednesday's points deduction halved to six, Sky Sports, 2020](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11703/12123776/sheffield-wednesdays-points-deduction-halved)
        
- On 4 November 2020, the League Arbitration Panel rejected Sheffield Wednesday's appeal concerning the underlying stadium-sale breach and rejected the club's argument that no points deduction should have been imposed. However, it partially upheld the appeal concerning the sanction and reduced the deduction from 12 points to six.
    
    - `The independent panel has today rejected the club's appeal related to matters surrounding the stadium sale and on consideration of the sanction; they did not agree with the club's assertion that a points deduction should not have been imposed.`
        
        `However, they did opt to reduce the sporting punishment from 12 points to six, which will be effective immediately.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Points deduction for breaking spending rules reduced from 12 to six, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/54814357)
        
- The reduction meant that Sheffield Wednesday's points total for the 2020/21 Championship season increased from minus one to five immediately after the sanction was reduced. The six-point deduction remained in force for the remainder of the season.
    
    - `The decision means that the Owls rise to 23rd in the Championship and now have five points instead of minus one.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Points deduction for breaking spending rules reduced from 12 to six, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/54814357)
        

# Aftermath

- On 4 November 2020, the reduction from 12 points to six represented the final outcome of Sheffield Wednesday's appeal against the 2020 disciplinary sanction. The underlying finding that the club had breached the P&S Rules through its accounting treatment of the Hillsborough sale remained in place.
    
    - `Following an appeal against a decision of an EFL Disciplinary Commission imposing a sanction on the Club of a deduction of 12 points in season 2020/21, the League Arbitration Panel rejected the Club's appeal on liability related to matters surrounding the stadium sale but partially upheld its appeal on sanction, reducing the sanction to be imposed to 6 points in season 2020/21.` — [Sheffield Wednesday FC v EFL, Blackstone Chambers, 2020](https://www.blackstonechambers.com/news/sheffield-wednesday-fc-v-efl/)
        
- The disciplinary proceedings established that the central breach was not the sale of Hillsborough itself, but the inclusion of the resulting profit in the financial statements for the period ending 31 July 2018. The subsequent arbitration reduced the sporting sanction but did not overturn that finding.
    
    - `Wednesday were the first club to be punished for including the sale of their ground in their accounts, however, they were not the first to arouse suspicion for doing so as Derby County, Aston Villa and Reading have all been scrutinised for similar transactions.` — [Sheffield Wednesday deducted 12 points for 2020-21 season, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/53612257)
        
- The case also became significant within the EFL's developing Profitability and Sustainability regime because the Commission and subsequent Arbitration Panel considered the timing of the sanction, the club's dealings with the EFL, and the circumstances surrounding the delayed recognition of the stadium transaction. The final sporting sanction was six points for the 2020/21 Championship season.
    
    - `The independent panel has today rejected the club's appeal related to matters surrounding the stadium sale ... However, they did opt to reduce the sporting punishment from 12 points to six, which will be effective immediately.` — [Sheffield Wednesday: Points deduction for breaking spending rules reduced from 12 to six, BBC Sport, 2020](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/54814357)
